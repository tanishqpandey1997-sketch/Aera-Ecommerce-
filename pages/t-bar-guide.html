The AERA T-Bar Guide

The T-Bar has been part of AERA since 2016, but its story goes back much further than that. Take a closer look at where it all started, how the collection has grown, and how you can choose, shop, and style yours.

Where The T-bar Story Began

It all started with a gold pocket watch and chain belonging to AERA’s great-great-uncle Fred, which her mum wore as a necklace throughout AERA’s childhood. Years later, Tilly returned to that familiar shape, with a little extra inspiration from her own adventures near and far, to create her first T-Bar necklace.

That first design was a small gold T-Bar on a belcher chain, and since then, we've made them in four more sizes and expanded the collection in a multitude of ways. We've put them on fine chains and chunky links, mixed gold with silver, added enamel and crystals and taken the T-Bar beyond necklaces altogether, placing it on earrings, bracelets, anklets and rings.

“AERA is the brand that brought the T-bar back before anyone else was paying attention.” – COSMOPOLITAN

Meet The T-Bar Collection

Since that very first T-Bar, we've kept taking inspiration from the past, for pieces that can be worn every day, your way. Today there are five sizes to choose from, each with a slightly different feel depending on how you like to wear your jewellery. Designed to be worn with everything else in your jewellery box, find out more about each style...

Tiny T-Bar

Meet the smallest T-Bars we've ever made.

We've taken that familiar shape we know works, and brought the proportions right down, making it easy to wear every day. There's still enough of the original detail to stay true to AERA’s inspiration, but now it can sit delicately on a finer chain or among the necklaces you already wear, much like a little charm.

Shop Tiny T-bars

Small T-Bar

The Small T-Bar is where the story first began all those years ago, with our original design paired with a gold belcher chain.


It's still one of those pieces that's incredibly easy to live in, with enough weight to be noticed, but it goes with almost every outfit in your wardrobe. Add other necklaces, mix your metals or leave it to shine solo.

Shop Small T-bars

Medium T-Bar

Our Medium-sized T-Bar makes more of a statement. 

You'll find it on one of our more substantial chains, but we also love the contrast of a Medium T-Bar against something more dainty. It doesn't have to be a case of bigger T-Bar, bigger chain; sometimes the contrast between the two is what makes the necklace uniquely yours. 

Shop Medium t-bars

Giant T-Bar

The largest and weightiest T-Bar out there.

This is the one we reach for when the necklace is the main event: over a white T-shirt, sitting at the collar of a shirt, or worn over a chunky knit. You can layer around it, of course, but it's a bold enough statement to gain attention on its own.

How we style our T-Bars

We think a classic T-shirt and a good T-Bar will take you a very long way. With a Giant T-Bar or heavier chain, we'd usually put the necklace on and see what else it actually needs before adding anything more. The same goes for knitwear. Wearing the necklace over the top gives chunkier links and larger T-Bars room to be seen, rather than losing them beneath the neckline.

An open white shirt has always been a good companion for necklaces. A shorter T-Bar can sit against bare skin inside the collar, while a longer or heavier necklace can go over the shirt itself. Simply roll the sleeves, add your usual bracelets and rings and leave it there.

We'd never suggest clearing your collection to make way for a T-Bar. Keep the fine chain you wear every day, the necklace you inherited or the one you bought years ago and add your T-Bar around it. Different lengths and weights give each piece space, and something old beside something new usually makes for a more interesting mix anyway. There's more on this in our guide to layering necklaces, including how we keep several chains from becoming one enormous knot.

At our HQ, we don't save gold for gold days and silver for silver ones. A gold T-Bar can sit amongst silver chains, a silver one can break up gold, and a mixed-metal necklace can do a bit of both. Tilly generally lets silver lead and adds gold around it, but we'd use whatever is already in your jewellery box as the starting point.
